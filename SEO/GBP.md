# Google Business Profile — Bryce's Patios

**Everything here is copy-paste ready. Set it up in this order, top to bottom.**
This is the single highest-value thing we can do for the business. For a local
contractor, a complete Google Business Profile outranks every other channel —
website included. It is free.

> **Short on time?** Do §1, §2, §3, §5 and §9 today. Come back for the rest.

**Setup URL:** https://business.google.com/add
**Do it from a phone, standing outside by the truck** — Google will ask for a verification
video and it has to be the real business location.

---

## 0. Read this first — three rules that prevent a suspension

1. **One profile. Ever.** Before you start, Google Maps → search `Bryce's Patios Mansfield`.
   If anything comes up, **stop and claim that listing instead.** Creating a second profile
   for the same business gets both of them banned, and getting unbanned is a multi-week fight.
2. **No keywords in the business name.** The name is `Bryce's Patios`. Not
   `Bryce's Patios Mansfield`, not `Bryce's Patios | Patios & Hardscaping`, not
   `Bryce's Patios LLC` unless the LLC is real and registered. Keyword-stuffed names are the
   #1 cause of contractor suspensions. The category and service areas do the ranking work,
   not the name.
3. **Never buy reviews, never post a fake address.** No P.O. box, no mailbox service, no
   "virtual office." Google's own guideline: P.O. boxes and remote mailboxes are not
   acceptable. Bryce's real address stays hidden (see §3) and the service areas do the
   geographic work.

## 8. Photos — the part most contractors skip, and the part that ranks

Google rewards profiles that upload consistently. **A listing with photos gets far more
clicks than one without.** Upload the first batch **the same day the profile is created** —
this alone flips a decent share of "near me" impressions.

**Upload order, highest impact first:**

| # | Slot | What to upload | Aspect ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Logo | The Bryce's Patios wordmark, transparent PNG | 1:1 |
| 2 | Cover | Best hero shot of a finished patio | 16:9 |
| 3 | Story / Exterior | Finished patio, full width, wide shot | 16:9 |
| 4–7 | At work | 4 finished-job photos, mix of wide + close-up stone detail | 4:3 or 1:1 |
| 8–11 | Before/after pairs | **The highest-converting photo type for hardscaping.** Same angle, both shots. | 4:3 |
| 12+ | Team / tools / truck | Real photos of Bryce's truck, tools, and job site | 4:3 or 1:1 |

**Technical spec Google accepts:** JPG or PNG, between **10 KB and 5 MB**, minimum **10 px**
on the short side for profile photos / **250 px** for cover. Aim for **≥ 1200 px wide** and at
least **50,000 total pixels** (e.g. 300×200) so it renders crisply in the carousel.

**Reality check:** the current site photography is AI-generated and disclosed on-page. Do not
upload AI images to Google as "photos of work here." Google's image policy requires photos to
represent the actual location/business. So: **the real job photos have to come from Bryce**
(wide shot + close-ups of the stone, from any finished job). Until those arrive, use only
photos of the truck, tools, and signage — the things that are literally real.

**Ongoing:** one new photo a week is enough to keep the profile "active." A single upload a
week beats 30 photos in one day.

## 9. Verification — the video is the whole game

Google verifies a new service-area business by asking for **a real video recorded on the
verification day.** You cannot do this part for Bryce. It must be him, on his phone, outside,
on the day Google asks for it. It is one take, no editing.

**What Google wants to see:** the street, the neighborhood, and proof this is a working
business address. In their own reinstatement guidance, the strongest evidence is **permanent
outdoor and indoor signage, a company vehicle with signage, a licence or registration, and a
business phone bill tied to the name and address.**

**Shoot list — walk it in this order, keep talking the whole time, keep the phone moving:**

1. Street sign at the corner of West St, then pan down the street to the driveway.
2. The truck, from the back, plates blurred if you like — then open the bed: wheelbarrow,
   plate compactor, shovels, rakes, level, tamper.
3. Hold up something with the business name on it — an estimate sheet, a card, a receipt book
   with `Bryce's Patios` written on it.
4. Unlock the truck with the keys, throw the work shirt on, grab a tool.
5. Ten seconds of actual work — spreading material, tamping, or laying stone.

**Say something like this out loud, in one breath:**

> "Hey, this is Bryce, Bryce's Patios, I build stone patios and retaining walls around
> Mansfield. This is the truck, this is my tools, and this is the neighborhood I work out of."

**Do not film:** bank or tax paperwork, IDs or licence numbers, other people's faces
(including family and customers), or anything inside the house.

**If Google offers a text or call code instead of video:** answer it immediately and read the
code back. Never let it go to voicemail.

**If verification fails:** tap **Contact support** and reply with the signage/truck photos.
**Do not create a second listing** — that is the thing that turns a fixable problem into a
banned business.

## 10. Q&A — seed these 10 immediately

The Questions & Answers block on a Google listing sits right under the photos and is
**publicly visible on the Maps profile.** Anyone can post a question, and anyone can answer —
including the owner. Most contractors leave it empty. Seeding it with the real questions
customers actually call and ask costs nothing and quietly wins jobs.

**Post each question yourself, then answer it as the owner.** Answers show as "Owner".

| Question to post | Answer to post |
|---|---|
| Do you give free estimates? | Yes. Estimates are free and there's no obligation. Text a photo of the yard to (508) 212-6433 and Bryce will get back to you the same day. |
| How much does a stone patio cost? | It depends on size, material, and how much digging the yard needs. A rough ballpark is $25–$60 per square foot installed. Text a couple of photos and the approximate size and Bryce will give you a real number. |
| How long does a patio take to build? | Most residential patios take three to seven working days once we start, weather depending. Base prep and drainage are the slow part, and they're the part that matters. |
| Do you travel to my town? | We cover southeastern Massachusetts, including Mansfield, Attleboro, Norton, Foxborough, Taunton, Easton, Sharon, Seekonk, Rehoboth, Plainville, and Franklin. If you're nearby, just ask. |
| Do you do the digging, or do I need to? | We handle everything: removal of the old surface, excavation, the compacted aggregate base, drainage, and the finished stone. |
| What stone do you work with? | Bluestone, flagstone, granite, and concrete pavers. We'll walk you through the difference in person — bluestone and flagstone look best, pavers cost less and hold up well to freeze-thaw. |
| Will my patio heave or sink in the winter? | Not if it's built right. Frost movement in Massachusetts is the main thing that ruins patios, and it's caused by a shallow or uncompacted base and trapped water. We dig deep, compact in lifts, and cut drainage. |
| Can you fix a patio someone else installed? | Yes. Re-levelling sunken pavers, rebuilding failing edges, and fixing drainage is a normal part of the work. |
| Are you licensed and insured? | *(leave unanswered until Bryce confirms — then answer honestly)* |
| What areas don't you cover? | Anything more than about two hours from Mansfield. Inside that, we'll show up. |

**Rules for Q&A:** never post a question and answer that claims a licence, insurance,
certification, warranty, or review that isn't real. Those are the claims that turn a good
listing into a liability. Answer customer questions within a day; speed of response is a
ranking signal.

## 11. Reviews — the actual ranking engine

Reviews are the strongest local ranking factor that Bryce fully controls, and the single
biggest driver of whether someone calls. **The goal for the first 90 days: 10 reviews.**

**The one rule:** ask every happy customer, the day the job ends, in person, with the phone
in your hand. Not a week later by email. Fast ask, high conversion.

### The direct review link

Once the profile is live, Google gives a short review link:

1. Business Profile dashboard → **Ask for reviews**
2. Copy the short link (looks like `https://g.page/r/XXXXXXXX/review`)
3. Save it as a **contact** in Bryce's phone called `Review link`, and text it to himself so
   it's one tap away forever

**Text message template — copy this:**

```
Hey [Name] — Bryce here, thanks again for having us out. If the patio
made you happy, would you mind leaving a quick Google review? It's what
gets a one-man shop found. Two sentences is plenty. Here's the link:
[review link]
```

**In-person ask, word for word:**

> "If you're happy with it, would you mind leaving me a Google review? I'll text you the
> link right now — it takes thirty seconds. It's genuinely how people find me."

Then **send the link before he leaves the driveway.** When the ask happens on site, with the
work in front of both of you, people say yes. A week later by email, most don't.

**Reply to every single review**, good and bad, within 48 hours. Replies are read by both
Google and the next customer.
- Five stars → thank them and name the job: *"Thanks Dana — that bluestone walkway came out great."*
- Three stars → acknowledge, no arguing, and give the phone number to fix it.
- One star → stay polite, keep it short, and invite them to call. Never argue publicly.

**Do not buy reviews, do not ask family, do not write them yourself.** Fake reviews get
filtered and, worse, can get the profile suspended. Ten real reviews beat fifty fake ones.

> **Once five real reviews exist**, we add a reviews section to the website and a
> `aggregateRating` to the structured data. Not before — inventing a rating is the fastest
> way to lose Google's trust.

## 12. Posts — one a week keeps the profile warm

Google Posts appear on the profile and expire after about a week, so this is a cadence, not a
one-time job. Once a week: one finished job, before/after, one photo, two sentences, and a
`Get an estimate` button. That's it. A profile with recent posts noticeably outranks an
abandoned one.

**Post template:**

```
Finished this [bluestone patio / paver walkway / retaining wall] in
[Town] this week. [One true sentence about what was tricky about it.]

Free estimates — text (508) 212-6433.
```
