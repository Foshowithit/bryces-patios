Land clearing and brush removal in Mansfield, MA

Free estimateCall Bryce

Most lots around here have a back corner that has not been touched in twenty years. Brush, saplings, vines and the pile of everything the last owner left get taken out, and what is underneath gets graded so the ground is usable instead of just empty.

Clearing is a question of what you want the ground to do afterwards. Opening a view is one job. Getting a yard level enough to put a patio on, or wide enough to park a trailer on, is a different one, and it decides how far the work goes and where the spoil ends up.

What comes out leaves the property unless you want it dealt with here. Brush and saplings can be chipped on site, and the chips are worth keeping as mulch if there is somewhere sensible to put them. Stumps get cut low, and grubbing the roots out is a separate decision that depends on whether anything is going to be built or planted over them.

The part most people do not see coming is the ground underneath. Clearing opens soil to light and water and it takes a season to settle. If something is planned for the space, the time to grade it and put the base down is after the roots are out, not in the same week as the brush comes down.

Boundaries get checked before anything is cut. A lot line is not always where the trees make it look, and a boundary survey is cheaper than putting the trees back.

Common questions

Land Clearing questions, answered

Do I need a permit to clear land in Massachusetts?

It depends on your town and on where on the lot the clearing sits. Work inside a wetland buffer, a floodplain or on a slope over the town's threshold usually needs a filing, and some towns restrict how much of a lot can be cleared at all. We check your town's rule before anything is cut.

Will clearing my lot disturb the ground?

Yes, and it is worth planning around. Opening a wooded area to light and water changes how the soil behaves for a season, and anything built right after clearing wants the roots gone and the base put down after that settles. If a patio or a shed is the plan, we clear and grade with that in mind in one pass.

What happens to the brush and the stumps?

Brush and saplings are chipped on site and the chips can stay as mulch if you have a place for them, otherwise they leave with the rest. Stumps are cut low; grinding them out is a separate step, and whether it needs doing depends on what will sit on that ground afterwards.
